Data: TradingView scanner, refreshed automatically (most exchanges are 15 min delayed on TradingView's free feed). Open Px = today's opening price (closed before that market opens). Purchase Px = your average price per share over all buys (free shares count at 0). Click ▸ next to a ticker for every transaction. Close Px = previous session close. Day % = the stock's move today; before its market opens (e.g. US stocks in the European morning) it shows closed and counts 0 in your Day figure. The Ext. hours switch adds pre-market and after-hours moves, marked with a green E; they never change your totals. Double-click any column title to rank by it (again to reverse, a third time to reset). Bench % = benchmark move since your open date. Header 1 Week / 1 Month = your portfolio's own return over that full period (see Performance chart for the two methods); if you have held it for less, it shows "–" and the return since opening underneath. Realized P&L = gains and losses from shares you sold + dividends. Dividends are booked automatically on their payment date when you held the shares before the ex-date (gross amount from TradingView, marked AUTO in the Dividends table of the Trades tab). Click a ticker or name to open its news, releases and earnings. Unrealized P&L = what would go into your P&L if you sold every open position at today's price. The per-stock 1W/1M/YTD/1Y/10Y columns are the stock's own moves; in the total row they are your portfolio's, shown only once it is older than the period. IPO Px = first available split-adjusted close on TradingView, used for Since IPO. When a stock listed after the start of a period, those cells merge into one box showing its IPO date. Performance chart: On capital (default) = your gain in euros so far (price moves, sales, dividends) ÷ the most money you had invested in the period, so the line has the shape of your P&L and, with no sales, ends at your P&L %; each benchmark is bought with the same money on the same dates and sold in the same proportions. TWR = time-weighted return, the standard for fund managers: it ignores how much money was in, so a small early buy that did well can make it look much higher than your P&L. Both also drive the header 1 Week / 1 Month and the total row. Pick one or more benchmarks in Benchmarks ▾ (your choice syncs to every device); lines use daily closes, plus hourly prices for the last month, saved by the news robot; each line is in its own currency, like your positions. The header 1 Week / 1 Month compare with the first benchmark you picked. Sector attribution = weight × (position return − its benchmark return), in percentage points, using TradingView sectors.
